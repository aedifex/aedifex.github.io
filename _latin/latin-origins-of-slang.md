## The hook

Slang feels like the newest layer of a language. A surprising amount of it is a very old layer, clipped and remixed.

## Street culture is old

- Pompeii's walls: graffiti, insults, boasts, election notices, love notes. Romans had street culture, and it was written on the street.
- *Graffiti* itself: Italian, from *graffiare*, "to scratch."

## Latin in the clip-and-remix

Modern slang loves to chop a long word down. Many of those long words are Latin.

- **sus** ← suspect ← *suspicere*, "to look up at, mistrust"
- **fam** ← family ← *familia*, "household"
- **rad** ← radical ← *radix*, "root"
- **vibe** ← vibration ← *vibrare*, "to shake"
- **delulu** ← delusional ← *deludere*, "to mock, deceive"
- **mob** ← *mobile vulgus*, "the fickle crowd" (17th-century slang that stuck)
- **bus** ← *omnibus*, "for everyone"

## Latin by way of the street

- **savvy** ← Portuguese/Spanish *sabe* ← *sapere*, "to know"
- **capisce** ← Italian *capire* ← *capere*, "to grasp"
- **pronto** ← Spanish/Italian ← *promptus*, "ready"
- **pleb** ← *plebs*, the common people, now an insult again

## The ones that aren't true

- *Sincere* from *sine cera*, "without wax": a famous false etymology.
- The point: the real history is better than the myths.

## The argument

Latin never died. It went to the street. To write.
